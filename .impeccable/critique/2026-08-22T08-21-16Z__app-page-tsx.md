## Design Specificity Verdict

The page feels authored for “nomi.” The vortex, mineral palette, typography, dark reading-room concept, and restrained motion are distinctive. The automated scan found no generic-pattern violations in `app/page.tsx`.

The weakness is compositional, which the detector cannot measure. The screenshot confirms that the header is technically aligned to the same 1120px shell, yet feels visually imported from another site because its wordmark and navigation are very small, it repeats the hero identity, and a full-width divider separates it from a hero that begins much farther down.

No reliable browser overlay was available because no browser backend was connected. The supplied 1918×860 screenshot and a healthy HTTP response from the local site were used as the visual fallback.

## Overall Impression

The user is right. The horizontal whitespace is disciplined; the vertical whitespace is excessive. The page is not empty because it lacks content—it feels empty because the hero is forced to consume an entire viewport and vertically centers a 450px card inside it. That leaves roughly 150px above and below the main composition while completely hiding “More writing.”

The top bar is also genuinely off, but not because its alignment is wrong. It belongs to a generic minimalist blog while the hero beneath it has a much stronger custom identity.

## What’s Working

- The learner/profile and latest-write-up pairing is fundamentally strong.
- The charcoal-teal featured surface removes glare without losing prominence.
- The vortex, Archivo/Public Sans pairing, mineral accents, and quiet animation create a recognizable identity without terminal-blog styling.

## Priority Issues

### P1 — Sample work is not disclosed on the homepage

All current posts are marked as sample content, but the featured homepage card and homepage catalogue do not show that status.

Why it matters: a CV or scholarship reviewer could assume these are completed personal analyses.

Fix: add “Sample write-up” to featured metadata and “Sample” to homepage catalogue rows.

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

### P2 — The hero consumes a full viewport without enough evidence

The full-viewport minimum, vertical centering, and padding create about 300px of combined empty vertical space and keep every other post below the fold.

Why it matters: the portfolio should demonstrate a learning trajectory, but the first screen shows one isolated example.

Fix: remove the full-viewport minimum, top-bias the composition, and reveal the “More writing” heading or first divider.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

### P2 — The header is aligned but perceptually detached

The 72px bar, tiny type, repeated vortex/name, full-width divider, and large gap before the hero make it feel like separate generic chrome.

Why it matters: it weakens the custom identity and fragments the page.

Fix: use a 58–64px quieter header, soften its divider, and let the hero own the full identity.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

### P2 — Two elements compete to be the primary focal point

The large “nomi” word and featured title have similar authority, while “Read write-up” is only 11px.

Why it matters: visitors are not strongly guided toward reading the work.

Fix: let the featured post lead, add a truthful label, and raise the reading action to 13–14px.

Suggested command: `$impeccable typeset`

### P3 — Small persistent details amplify the unfinished feeling

The 10–11px metadata and placeholder links are fragile, and the bright teal scrollbar is unusually prominent.

Fix: raise important metadata to 12px, soften the scrollbar, and hide blank profile links until populated.

Suggested command: `$impeccable polish`
